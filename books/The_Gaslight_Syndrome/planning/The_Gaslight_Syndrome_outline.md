# The Gaslight Syndrome — Outline

## Premise

Dr. Eleanor Voss, a clinical psychologist who specializes in coercive control, treats Claire Templeton, whose husband Thomas she believes is dismantling her reality through reality distortion, intermittent reinforcement, isolation, and triangulation. As Claire keeps a notebook of the "weather" of her marriage, Eleanor begins to notice the same signatures in her own office, files, and home. What appears to be a private crime becomes a network — fake clinics, staged appointments, anonymous letters, and an archive of witness statements that blur the line between record and reality. Eleanor must defeat the network by turning its own techniques against it.

## Act I — Sessions 1–8 (Chapters 1–12)

Claire arrives for intake, afraid that she is losing her mind. Over weekly sessions she describes moved objects, contradicted memories, 2:14 a.m. texts, diagonal kitchen chairs around a square head chair, and husband Thomas who never shouts but who "manages" every conversation. Eleanor names the patterns of coercive control and teaches Claire to keep two true sentences in a freezer drawer. Meanwhile Eleanor finds her own manuscript pages altered, her diary annotated, her referrals fabricated. She begins to keep her own archive and to photograph her office after hours.

## Act II — The Clinic in the Files (Chapters 13–28)

Claire's testimony leads Eleanor to a fake practice at Bethwood, to Alfred Black's record firm, and to the silver postbox on Beech Street. The archive grows: unsigned appointments, consent templates, a shoebox of letters, staged chairs, a witness named Simone whose "notes" read like the manual. Eleanor confronts Thomas in managed conversations, learns that Alfred Black is dying, and begins to suspect the manipulation is not a marriage but an industry. She starts keeping her own records under the same methods she teaches: pages, photographs, inventories, witnesses.

## Act III — The Network Writes Back (Chapters 29–49)

The archive turns against itself. Eleanor receives staged appointments in her own name, fake reviews from her own editor, intercepted letters from patients. Alfred Black's "record firm" sends her a letter in her own handwriting. Claire's sister is targeted; Thomas proposes to "manage" Claire's recovery at the same clinic. Eleanor and Claire counter with the manipulators' own grammar: intermittent silence, staged meetings where the recorder runs, triangulating Thomas through his own witness, and depositing depositions with the attorney where his scripts cannot reach. Alfred Black's documents are recovered; the shoebox surfaces; the clinic's archive is seeded.

## Act IV — The Grand Review (Chapters 50–67)

The archive is opened at Bethwood. Confrontations occur at the mirror shop, the clinic, and the lake. The firm writes back; Claire counts chairs; the fake postbox is logged, then dropped. Eleanor writes the chapter she promised about a patient's archive being her own, in her own hand, no longer crossed like Thomas's. Claire chooses her round table; the freezer drawer moves from evidence to filing system; the clock above the door keeps its slow kindness. The story closes with the archive's line about what manipulation makes of testimony — and the telling becomes the telling.
