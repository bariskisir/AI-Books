# The Social Engineer — Outline

**Genre:** Crime / Techno Thriller (Heist / Con Artist)
**Protagonist:** Alex Mercer, 29, elite social engineer — steals secrets, not money.

## Act I (Ch. 1–14): The Job
- Alex infiltrates Halcyon Biotech as a warm-up (Ch. 1), establishing the rules of the trade: never steal from anyone smaller than you; the mark is always the expert; the con ends with the mark believing they acted alone.
- A mysterious client, "Merchant," hires her to infiltrate data-broker conglomerate Helix Dyne — 280 million psychological profiles, election manipulation as a product. Her own file is in the system, eerily accurate.
- She works her way in (badges, tailgating, the Analyst Three cover), befriends sysadmin Marcus Chen, and reaches floor eleven — where Priya Voss, Helix's enigmatic chief, is waiting for her. Voss makes her an offer. Alex refuses; Voss points her at "the studio" (the election-messaging shop).

## Act II (Ch. 15–44): The Mirror
- Alex reads her own file: defection potential, split loyalties, "will accept the offer if the offer is right."
- Eli, her handler, reveals Helix found the leak — and him. Client turns cold. Voss offers everything; Alex walks away with the file, then returns it, then spirals through the fallout (the middle chapters chart her guilt loop: the ledger's categories — *the usable ones, the losers, the ones to call* — the old man in the fourth ward she once cold-called, the umbrella/rain reckoning).
- She tries to expose it: writes the story, tells everyone. The pollster "helps." On election day the story runs — and dies: Helix salted her draft with three planted errors and debunked it. She was never the burglar; she was the locksmith hired to test the doors. Merchant was Crane's audit contractor. Her flat is stripped of paper; they leave the cat.

## Act III (Ch. 45–77): The Counter-Con
- **Regroup (45–48):** Rule seven — count who told the house. Eli confesses he sold one detail (her first-month motel) to delete his daughter's address from the ledger; he brings the money trail: the Quiet Partnership and its founder, Silas Crane — the one person the machine never profiles. Alex finds the blind spot: the file is all past tense. *They can't profile a person who is changing.* Rule eight: if they're watching the doors, be the house.
- **Backstory (49):** The Old Gig — six years earlier, a campaign phone bank; the old man's call; the tapes Helix bought. The file wasn't smarter than her memories; it just wouldn't forget.
- **Inside (50–56):** Alex returns as a "converted asset" (Senior Consultant, Narrative Integrity), performs her own file daily. Crew: Mei Okafor (model integrity), Marcus (fired, outside tech), River (comms desk), Arun, Eli (printing). Whitaker the pollster is Crane's narrative director — a dangerous witness. Mei designs the "Correction": a scientifically defensible recalibration that makes Helix's persuasion 4% off human. Merchant gives her the Zurich Census: the annual client review Crane attends in person.
- **Zurich (57–66):** Alex infiltrates as catering staff ("Sylvie Marchand"). At the Census she performs the audit as a live product demo; Crane tests her — profile Voss, live. Alex delivers the file's read (loyal, negligible defection risk), keeping Voss beneath suspicion. That night Voss flips: she hands over the paper ledger (client contracts, floor-eleven overrides) and signs the Correction. "It can't model a person changing. I want to watch it be wrong."
- **The Mailing (67–69):** Marcus pulls the working lists; Eli and Doyle's rotary press print 41,000 files, mailed from 41 towns with a hand-set cover letter: *This is you, as they see you.* River posts the template and portal from the corporate account: *Ask us who you are.* Eleven client files land at the Census breakfast.
- **Climax (70–71):** The clients read themselves and walk. The Correction makes Helix's counter-messaging land as self-parody. Crane, alone with Alex, receives his own file — written by her by hand: *Predicted response: he will make you an offer.* He makes the offer. She declines. The mark unwinds the company himself, believing it's damage control.
- **Resolution (72–77):** Commissions, suspended campaigns, court-ordered deletion of the model (witnessed by Alex, Mei, Arun). Crane resigns and sends her his annotated file: *You missed a spot.* She doesn't write back. She declines the commission job, the rival firm, the memoir; the pollster writes the self-serving bestseller instead. She makes peace with the old man ("You were the umbrella." "I know. I put it down."). Eli retires toward his daughter's street. Alex appends one line to her own file — *Subject is aware of the observer. Subject changed.* — deletes the last copy, and buys cilantro at the market like a person in no one's ledger.

## Themes
- Identity vs. profile: the machine knows who you were, never who you're becoming.
- The con as empathy weaponized — and empathy as the only way out of it.
- Paper vs. data: what burns in the order you choose.
